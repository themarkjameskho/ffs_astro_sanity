01
What This Site Is
Purpose, audience, and the two build configurations

This is an SEO-first lead generation website for a bed bug treatment business. Its goal is to rank in both traditional Google search and Google Maps local pack results, then convert that traffic into quote requests and phone calls. Every page type exists to serve one of those two goals — or both simultaneously.

Single-Operator Configuration
One physical location, one service area. The homepage IS the GBP (Google Business Profile) landing page and must satisfy all local SEO requirements. GBP links directly to the homepage. Simpler architecture — approximately 44 pages before blog content for a location serving 6 surrounding cities.
Franchise / Multi-Location Configuration
Multiple physical locations, each with its own GBP listing. The homepage is a brand hub and location router — it does not receive any GBP link. Each location has its own dedicated location page that GBP links to. Page count multiplies per location added.
Configuration Choice Affects the Homepage and Contact Page Most
The six core page types (LOC-A, LOC-B, SP, SLP, CP, Blog) work the same in both configurations. The homepage and contact page have meaningfully different requirements depending on which configuration applies. Each individual spec sheet calls out variant differences where they exist.

URL Conventions — Apply Sitewide
Lowercase only · Hyphens not underscores · Trailing slash consistent throughout · State and city slugs use full names (not abbreviations) · No parameters or query strings in canonical URLs · Service slugs are fixed: heat-treatment · aprehend-treatment · chemical-treatment · canine-inspection · bed-bug-inspection

The Two-Click Rule
Every LOC-A page must be reachable within 2 clicks from the homepage. Every LOC-B and SLP page must be reachable within 1 click from their parent LOC-A or SP page. No money page should be more than 3 clicks from the homepage. If the franchise has many locations, a /locations/ hub page between the homepage and LOC-A pages is acceptable.

06
The Two SEO Systems
The site runs two overlapping hub-and-spoke structures — understanding both is essential before building
System 1 — Service Hub
SP → SLP spokes
Each Service Page (SP) is the hub. Its spokes are SLP pages — one per city for that service. The SP passes authority down; each SLP links back up. This system targets "service + city" queries.
SP → SLP [City 1] · SLP [City 2] · SLP [City N]
System 2 — Location Hub
LOC-A → LOC-B + SLP spokes
Each LOC-A page is the hub for its service area. Its spokes are LOC-B proximity pages and SLP pages for that city. This system targets "exterminator near [city]" and local map pack queries.
LOC-A → LOC-B ×N + SLP ×5
Where the Two Systems Intersect
SLP pages are the intersection point. They receive a link from their parent SP page (service system) AND from their city's LOC-A page (location system). This dual inbound authority is what makes SLP pages the strongest converting pages in the site. Do not break either link — both are required per the individual SLP spec.
07
The Unique Content Rule
The single most important quality constraint across the entire page set
Three Page Types Require Genuinely Unique Intro Content — No Templates
LOC-B, SLP, and Blog posts each require a unique opening section that cannot be replicated across pages by swapping a city name. Google actively targets geo-templated doorway pages. Pages that copy the same structure with only the city name changed will not rank — and may trigger a quality penalty that suppresses the entire domain.
Page TypeWhat Must Be UniqueMinimumWhat Can Be Consistent
LOC-BIntro paragraph — local neighborhood, housing type, landmark, drive-time from GBP city100–150 wordsServices section, process section, FAQ, CTA structure
SLPIntro paragraph — why this specific treatment suits this city's housing type, local reference100–150 wordsHow it works (brief), what to expect, pricing, FAQ structure
BlogFull article — original analysis, not AI-spun rewrites of other posts600+ wordsPost template, CTA block, schema structure
Pages that do NOT require unique intro content: LOC-A, SP, and CP pages are each one-of-a-kind by definition — there is only one per city (LOC-A), one per service (SP), and one sitewide (CP). Their content must be accurate and complete, but uniqueness across multiple instances is not a concern.

How to Use This Spec Set
Starting a new page? Find its page type, open the corresponding spec sheet, and work through it section by section. The wireframe section shows what to build. The checklist at the end confirms it's done correctly.

Reviewing a finished page? Use the pre-launch checklist at the end of the relevant spec sheet. Every item must pass before the page goes live.

Not sure which page type applies? Check Section 02 of this document — the "At a Glance" cards describe each type's role and distinguishing characteristics. If a page covers one service in one city, it's an SLP. If it covers all services for one city, it's a LOC-A or LOC-B. If it covers one service nationally, it's an SP.
